Slay the Spire 2 Tier List — Every Card Ranked (v0.111.x Meta)

Full Slay the Spire 2 card tier list for all five characters: S through D rankings from the Mobalytics card table plus Baalorlord's card-by-card Ironclad review. Updated per patch.

Here is the current Slay the Spire 2 card meta at a glance: the tables below rank every card in the game, S through D, for all five characters plus the multiplayer-only pool, compiled from the Mobalytics community card table (pulled September 2026) cross-checked against Baalorlord's full card-by-card Ironclad review. This is a living page — balance swings every patch, so check the updated date above and the Patch Notes before you lock a run.

How to read the tiers: S = premium, take it almost always · A = strong, usually correct · B = solid role-player · C = situational, often a trap · D = avoid without a specific plan.

Ironclad S-tier, explained (from the full card-by-card review): Bloodletting is the best energy card on the character — reusable, and the HP cost pays for itself; Colossus is the best defensive card in the game when the enemy is Vulnerable (50% less damage taken, doesn't exhaust, stacks); Dominate is the best Strength gain — Strength for every Vulnerability on the enemy plus an immediate stack, so it multiplies itself; Battle Trance draws three at zero cost; Pommel Strike combines damage and draw. Note the source variance: the video review puts Evil Eye in S (block doubles when you exhausted a card this turn), while the community table has it in B — rankings this close to the line move patch to patch. Two v0.111.0 notes: Juggernaut was buffed (5(7)→6(8) damage) after landing in D, so expect it to climb; Reflect-style nerfs haven't touched Ironclad's core. Full build paths: Ironclad Builds.

Tank deserves its own warning: you take double damage so allies take half — in a two-player run that's a net loss for the team, and even at four players it usually just means you die first. The review's own words: it "sucks" and is expected to be reworked.

So Who's the Best Character?

Community consensus right now: the Regent is arguably the strongest of the two new characters, with Star Engine builds the most consistent path at high Ascension — while the Sovereign Blade offers the biggest single-hit highs at lower consistency. On the returning side, Ironclad's Dominate engine and Silent's draw-manipulation wall give them the most stable S-tier depth.

Editorial rankings disagree, usefully: DualShockers' July 2026 character ranking puts Defect first ("currently the best character... when you roll the optimal build for this guy, however, nothing can stand opposed to you"), Necrobinder second ("a huge fan favorite" — Osty plus the Doom status effect), Silent third, Regent fourth — with the caveat that the Regent carries the highest learning curve and plays like a Glass Cannon — and Ironclad fifth. Character rankings are the most subjective slice of the meta; the card tables above are the stabler reference. All of this moves with every bi-weekly patch — when it moves, this page's updated date moves with it.

Relic Notes (Quick Hits)

  • Very Hot Cocoa — start each combat with four extra Energy; S-tier for literally anyone.
  • Divine Destiny (Regent) — six Stars at combat start, but only from the upgraded starter relic slot.
  • Dusty Tomb — the relic that grants The Sealed Throne; the single strongest Regent outcome available.
  • Unceasing Top — combos with self-returning attacks (Shining Strike lines) into near-infinite turns.
  • Frozen Egg — Powers enter your deck upgraded; it turns Child of the Stars and Genesis online immediately.
