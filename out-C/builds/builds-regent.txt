19:T3f44,<p>The Regent is the second brand-new character in Slay the Spire 2 — an arrogant, star-headed alien royal whose kit runs on two mechanics: <strong>Stars</strong>, a secondary resource you generate with some cards and spend on stronger ones, and <strong>Forge</strong>, which permanently grows his Sovereign Blade&#39;s damage during a fight. This guide covers the four archetypes the material supports: <strong>Starfall</strong> (the star nuke), <strong>star mid-range</strong> (&quot;good stuff&quot;), the <strong>Forge</strong> sword build, and <strong>small-deck infinites</strong>.</p>
<h2>How the Regent Works</h2>
<p><strong>Stars</strong> are a secondary resource generated by other cards and expended to cast more powerful ones; his card pool splits into star generation and star payoffs. His starting relic, <strong>Crown of Stars</strong>, grants 3 Stars at the start of every combat — and its upgraded version, which starts you at 6 Stars, is described as one of the best upgraded starting relics in the game because it lets you cast real payoffs on turn one.</p>
<p><strong>Forge</strong> is tied to the Sovereign Blade (base 10 damage, costs 2, has Retain). Cards that say &quot;Forge X&quot; add X damage to the blade for the duration of the fight, and it stacks: Forge 10 twice means the blade hits for 30+. The first Forge card you play spawns the blade into your hand — but if it has already spawned, Forge will not spawn it again, so you rarely play the blade more than once or twice per combat.</p>
<p>Mobalytics&#39; character guide lists his strengths as: very powerful when mastered, strong combo potential, and access to Retain and card generation. His weaknesses: a lack of reliable draw, and resources that are difficult to manage and micro. Community play (Caleb Gannon&#39;s character guide) adds one more crucial truth: the Regent&#39;s card quality is extremely bimodal — he has a higher density of outright excellent cards than anyone, but also a higher density of unplayable ones, with very little in between. Skipping draft picks is normal and healthy on this character. It also means <strong>cards matter more than relics</strong>: push for card rewards and hit shops until you have a functional core, then look for relic support.</p>
<p>A quick roadmap: Act 1 is precarious — take early damage and Block, grab max HP, and don&#39;t feel bad about resting. You come online in Act 2, and a successful build makes Act 3 the easiest of any character in the game.</p>

<h2>Build 1: Starfall — the Star Nuke</h2>
<p><strong>Play pattern:</strong> stockpile Stars and Vigor across a turn or two, then cash everything in at once — most reliably through <strong>Radiate</strong>, which deals damage to every enemy for each Star you gained this turn (3 damage per Star by default). With enough setup the video shows Radiate going from 3 to 52 damage in a single turn after banking 25 Stars. If you&#39;ve played Magic: The Gathering, it plays like a storm deck: build up, then kill everything in one go. Starfall is the most reliable archetype community play has found — the backup payoff is <strong>Stardust</strong>, a spend-X damage card (cited at 5 damage per hit), weaker in multi-enemy fights.</p>

<p><strong>Relics:</strong> the material&#39;s relic advice for the Regent is thin — cards simply matter more. Crown of Stars (3 Stars at combat start, 6 upgraded) is the engine every Starfall plan uses, and the explicit advice is to push card rewards and shops first, relic slots second.</p>
<p><strong>How to pilot it:</strong> the dream line is Decisions, Decisions into Royal Gamble — roughly 27 Stars in one turn, which kills almost anything with a small damage boost. Stack Vigor patiently (Patter, Terraforming), don&#39;t attack while banking it, and block until the big turn. If Radiate never shows up, <strong>Lunar Blast</strong> is a worse version that only makes sense with Decisions, Decisions (doubled into itself, replaying Monologue/Terraforming for Strength — the video&#39;s best case reached ~600 damage) or as an infinite-deck payoff.</p>
<p><strong>Strengths and weaknesses:</strong> scales to absurd numbers and crushes bosses; but it leans on specific payoffs, and without Radiate/Royal Gamble you need a lot of sustained generation to get there.</p>
<h2>Build 2: Star Mid-Range (&quot;Star Good Stuff&quot;)</h2>

Many Star payoffs cost no Energy, so your Energy goes to Block while zero-cost payoffs handle offense. <strong>Void Form</strong> supercharges everything: the first two cards you play each turn are free, <em>including their Star costs</em>.</p>

<p><strong>Strengths and weaknesses:</strong> forgiving and flexible, with great Act 2–3 tempo; it depends on hitting the character&#39;s high-end rares, so skip aggressively — the bimodal pool punishes mediocre picks.</p>
<h2>Build 3: Forge — the Sovereign Blade One-Shot</h2>
<p><strong>Play pattern:</strong> play Forge cards to grow the Sovereign Blade (base 10 damage, cost 2, Retain) into a boss-killing sword, then one-shot something. Fair warning from every source: this is the archetype to avoid as a plan A. The blade rarely gets replayed in a combat, some bosses shut it down, and the rate is poor — Spoils of Battle spends a whole card to make the blade a 20-damage attack, while a plain common does 18 damage unupgraded by itself.</p>

<p><strong>Strengths and weaknesses:</strong> spectacular one-shot kills and a unique playstyle; but it&#39;s the least reliable build here — slow, easily hosed by certain bosses, and out-scaled by the character&#39;s own commons.</p>
<h2>Build 4: Small-Deck Infinites</h2>
<p><strong>Play pattern:</strong> the Regent has some of the best targeted exhaust in the game. Shrink your deck aggressively, survive on transformed Block, then loop <strong>Alignment</strong> and <strong>Glow</strong> to draw your entire deck every turn and win with whatever payoff you kept. Infinites are described as &quot;quite easy to accomplish&quot; for this character — you mainly need GUARDS!!! to make it work.</p>

<p><strong>Relics:</strong> none are specifically recommended for this archetype in the material — the same &quot;cards first&quot; rule applies.</p>
<p><strong>How to pilot it:</strong> aim for a deck around 16 cards or fewer; at that size GUARDS!!! can assemble the infinite almost by itself. Use the 0-cost Block minions it generates to stall while CHARGE!! and BEGONE! remove the last few cards, then draw your deck and spend the surplus Stars on your finisher.</p>
<p><strong>Strengths and weaknesses:</strong> the strongest late-game ceiling of the four and very repeatable once assembled; but it&#39;s draft-dependent — without GUARDS!!! (or heavy removal) the plan doesn&#39;t come together.</p>
<h2>Which Build Should You Pick?</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Starfall</strong> if you want the character&#39;s best raw power and enjoy a one-big-turn playstyle.</li>
<li><strong>Star mid-range</strong> if you&#39;d rather just play good cards — the safest route.</li>
<li><strong>Forge</strong> only if Seeking Edge appears early; it&#39;s a real but fragile archetype.</li>
<li><strong>Infinites</strong> if you draft GUARDS!!! and want the strongest Act 3 in the game.</li>
</ul>
<p>Whichever you pick: skip mediocre cards freely, prioritize card rewards over relics, and expect a rough Act 1 before a dominant Act 3.</p>
