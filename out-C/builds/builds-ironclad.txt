19:T1635,<p>The Ironclad wins Slay the Spire 2 through <strong>Vulnerability</strong>. The character&#39;s best card, Dominate, gains Strength for every Vulnerability on the enemy — which turns an old status debuff into the fuel for a scaling engine. This page covers both complete build paths: the <strong>Dominate engine</strong> (the consistent pick) and the <strong>Exhaust/Block engine with Body Slam</strong> (the tank-kill alternative).</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Transparency note: the dedicated community build posts for Ironclad were blocked (403) at collection time, so this page is built from the full card-by-card Ironclad review (Baalorlord&#39;s tier breakdown, ~91k views) cross-checked against the community card table — see the <a href="/cards/tier-list">Tier List</a> for the complete rankings.</p>
</blockquote>
<h2>Archetype 1: The Dominate Engine (Vulnerability → Strength)</h2>
<p>The plan: apply Vulnerability, then convert it. <strong>Dominate</strong> gives you one Strength for each Vulnerability on the enemy <em>plus</em> an immediate Vulnerability — one card that adds both flat damage and multiplies your total damage. Multiple copies scale with each other: more Vuln application means more Strength means bigger hits that apply more Vuln.</p>

<p><strong>The Vuln appliers that feed Dominate:</strong> Bash (your starter, C-tier but functional), <strong>Tremble</strong>, <strong>Thunderclap</strong> (Vuln on <em>all</em> enemies — sets up multi-target turns), and <strong>Molten Fist</strong> in a pinch. Once two or more Vulnerability stacks are on the target, <strong>Bully</strong> — a zero-cost Attack that scales its damage with the target&#39;s Vulnerability count — starts hitting like a premium card.</p>
<h2>Archetype 2: Exhaust / Block Engine with Body Slam</h2>
<p>The alternative plan: build an exhaust loop that generates enormous Block, then kill with <strong>Body Slam</strong> — an Attack that hits for your current Block, and upgrades to <strong>zero cost</strong>.</p>
<p><strong>The engine core:</strong></p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Card</th>
<th>Role</th>
</tr>

<td>Gain Block whenever a card is exhausted — the archetype&#39;s backbone</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Evil Eye (B/S*)</td>
<td>Eight Block, doubled if you exhausted a card this turn — &quot;ludicrously efficient&quot; when triggered</td>
</tr>

<td>Burning Pact (A)</td>
<td>Draw two plus exhaust a card — fuels and filters at once</td>
</tr>

<td>Exhaust a card for Block; upgrade it to choose the target</td>
</tr>
<tr>

<td>Damage that scales with your exhaust pile — &quot;easy to see this doing 100 damage&quot;</td>
</tr>
<tr>

<td>One-time big Energy if you exhausted this turn</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Body Slam (C→win condition)</td>
<td>Hits for your Block; upgraded it costs zero — a deck can win with this as its only Attack</td>
</tr>

<p><strong>How it plays:</strong> exhaust cheaply and repeatedly (True Grit, Burning Pact, status-cleansing), bank Block through Feel No Pain and Evil Eye, then slam. <em>The asterisk on Evil Eye: the video review ranks it S-tier, the community table B — it&#39;s exactly the kind of card whose stock rises the longer a run goes.</em></p>
<h2>The Strikes Strategy (Why Ironclad Keeps His Deck)</h2>
<p>Uniquely among the five characters, the Ironclad <strong>wants his starting Strikes</strong>: Strength and Vulnerability scale them, <strong>Strike Dummy</strong> multiplies them, and enchantments can make them free (the guide&#39;s example: a soup enchantment that makes every Strike cost zero, then draw your whole deck and play them all). Don&#39;t auto-remove Strikes the way you would on other characters.</p>
<h2>What to Avoid</h2>
<p>From the current card table&#39;s D tier and the review&#39;s C-tier warnings: <strong>Havoc</strong> (plays a random card from the top of your deck — can wipe your hand), <strong>Sword Boomerang</strong> (random-target damage that needs support it can&#39;t find), <strong>Demon Form</strong> (too slow for the current meta), <strong>Barricade</strong> and — before the v0.111.0 buff — <strong>Juggernaut</strong> (5(7)→6(8) damage; D-tier cards don&#39;t usually get real buffs like this, so watch it climb).</p>
<h2>Patch Sensitivity</h2>
<p>Ironclad&#39;s core (Bloodletting, Dominate, Colossus, Battle Trance) has been stable across recent patches, but this is a bi-weekly-balance game — check the <a href="/news/patch-notes">Patch Notes</a> and the <a href="/cards/tier-list">Tier List</a> <code>updated</code> date before you commit. For the other four characters, see the <a href="/builds/builds-overview">Builds overview</a>.</p>
