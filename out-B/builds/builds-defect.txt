19:T4258,<p>The Defect returns in Slay the Spire 2 as the Spire&#39;s master of elemental Orbs, rebuilt with &quot;a bulkier, more advanced chassis&quot;, a new Glass Orb type, and the Channel/Evoke engine you know. It also has what the community guide calls the best starting deck of any character — Dualcast plus guaranteed damage from your starting Lightning Orb. This page covers the archetypes the material supports: the <strong>frost tank</strong>, <strong>Big Zap lightning</strong>, <strong>Dark boss-killing</strong>, <strong>Claw zero-spam</strong>, and the secondary <strong>power-spam / energy toolbox / status</strong> routes.</p>
<h2>How the Defect Works</h2>
<p>You start with 3 Orb slots (expandable to 10–12). Every Orb has a passive effect each turn and an Evoke effect; newly Channeled Orbs spawn in the leftmost slot and push the others over, and an Orb pushed off the front gets Evoked. The Orb types, per the video guide:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Lightning</strong> — 3 damage per turn passively; Evoke deals 8 damage to a random enemy.</li>
<li><strong>Frost</strong> — Block, whether it&#39;s sitting in a slot or Evoked.</li>
<li><strong>Dark</strong> — grows by 6 damage every turn passively, only does something when Evoked, and hits the lowest-HP enemy (a real drawback against a boss with a minion).</li>
<li><strong>Plasma</strong> — 1 extra Energy per turn per Plasma Orb; Evoking one gives 2 Energy.- <strong>Glass</strong> (new in StS2) — 4 damage to all enemies per turn, decaying by 1 at the start of your turn; Evoking deals twice its current passive to all enemies. A faster-cycling, AoE-flavored alternative to Lightning.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Focus</strong> increases every number on your Orbs by 1 — permanent Focus is rare, but per-turn Focus (Synchronize and friends) can reach around 8 per turn. Orb slots × Focus is the character&#39;s multiplicative scaling engine. Your starting relic, <strong>Cracked Core</strong>, Channels 1 Lightning Orb at combat start: 3 guaranteed damage per turn no matter what your deck does, and Dualcast turns it into a 1-mana 16-damage burst that one-shots most of the earliest fights.</p>
<p>Mobalytics lists diverse playstyles, strong Powers, unique Orb mechanics and post-combat healing as strengths; less burst damage, cards that juggle immediate benefit against long-term sustainability, and some RNG as weaknesses. Two running themes from the video guide: <strong>remove Strikes</strong> (your passives do the damage — Strikes are the deck&#39;s worst card), and base damage is so high the Defect can afford early Block cards earlier than anyone. The run shape: Act 1 is easy, Act 2 is the hardest (pick up real scaling tools), and Act 3 is the easiest — 10 Frost Orbs blocking every turn simply cannot be killed through.</p>
<h2>Build 1: Frost Tank (Orb Slots + Focus)</h2>
<p><strong>Play pattern:</strong> Channel a wall of Frost Orbs, add Orb slots and Focus, and become unkillable while your Orbs grind the enemy down. Frost is called the strongest Orb type — Block is the most important resource, and Frost generation that blocks forever without spending Energy is &quot;crazy&quot;.</p>

<h2>Build 5: Secondary Scaling Routes (Powers, Energy Toolbox, Status)</h2>
<p>Three more viable scaling routes, ranked just behind Orb slots + Focus by the video guide:</p>
<p><strong>Power spam.</strong> <strong>Echo Form</strong> is a single-card scaling win condition (&quot;take an Echo Form and you can win with whatever is in your deck&quot;), Creative AI spawns random Powers every turn, and <strong>Signal Boost</strong> copies your next Power and persists across turns — play it early, then double your Echo Form three turns later. Power-synergy relics (Lost Wisp, Game Piece, Jeweled Mask) pay off here.</p>
<p><strong>Energy toolbox.</strong> The video guide&#39;s favorite: <strong>Turbo</strong> (+2 Energy, +3 upgraded), <strong>Super Critical</strong> (gain 4, Exhausts), <strong>Skim</strong> (draw), and <strong>Hologram</strong> to retrieve whichever you need. Turbo + Hologram + Skim is called &quot;a deck on its own&quot;, so all three sit in S tier. Meteor Strike feeds you Plasma (Energy every turn), and the surplus funds X-costs like Tempest or Helix Drill, or pseudo-infinite turns playing your whole deck two or three times. Bigger decks make Hologram better, not worse.</p>
<p><strong>Status package.</strong> Cards that create statuses are a real archetype now: <strong>Trash to Treasure</strong> Channels a random Orb whenever you create a status (&quot;probably the best card for the archetype&quot;), Smoke Stack deals 5 damage to all enemies per status created, Boost Away makes the statuses, and Rocket Punch becomes free when a status shows up. It&#39;s high variance — the reliable version builds around Turbo and Overclock rather than pure status spam.Compact (turns statuses in hand into Block) and Flak Cannon (Exhausts every status card in any zone) shine as <em>answers</em> to enemies that inflict Infection or Burns.</p>
<h2>Which Build Should You Pick?</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Frost tank</strong> for safety — the build that makes Act 3 trivial.</li>
<li><strong>Big Zap</strong> for tempo; <strong>Dark</strong> when you keep losing boss fights.</li>
<li><strong>Echo Form / energy toolbox</strong> when the Orb pieces don&#39;t come — they&#39;re almost always findable.</li>
<li><strong>Claw</strong> for nostalgia, with the honest caveat that StS2 rates it well below the Orb plans.</li>
</ul>
