Slay the Spire 2 launches Early Access with **five playable characters**: three returning from the first game (Ironclad, Silent, Defect) and two brand-new ones (Regent, Necrobinder). Each has its own card pool, relics and a mechanical identity that actually changes how you draft. Here's what every class plays like, with links to the full build guides.

## Ironclad — the self-damage scaling bruiser (returning)

The Ironclad remains the straightforward starter: trade health for value, stack Strength, and out-scale the fight. In Slay the Spire 2 his identity shifts in two notable ways:

- **Vulnerability is a resource, not just a debuff.** Cards like **Dominate** gain Strength for every Vulnerability on the enemy, which turns old vuln-appliers (Bash, Tremble, Molten Fist) into enablers for a scaling engine.
- **Strikes are worth keeping.** With enchantments (his Strikes can be made free) and self-synergies, the Ironclad wants his starting deck more than any other character.

Full card-by-card rankings and two complete build paths: [Ironclad Builds](/builds/builds-ironclad).

## Silent — the shiv-and-evasion specialist (returning)

The Silent still wins by doing everything at once: discard-tank, poison, and a dense shiv package. The current card pool leans hard into **draw-and-discard manipulation** — cards like **Well-Laid Plans**, **Tools of the Trade**, **Calculated Gamble** and **Prepared** sit in S/A tier alongside the usual **Footwork**-powered evasion. Build guide: [Silent Build](/builds/builds-silent).

## Defect — the orb engine (returning)

The Defect's orb framework is intact — channel, evoke, and scale with focus — and the S tier of the current card table reads like an orb checklist: **Echo Form**, **Defragment**, **Glacier**, **Loop**, **Capacitor**, **Overclock**, **Ice Lance** and **Chill**. Defragment-style focus stacking plus the new **Machine Learning** and **Genetic Algorithm** powers make the late game extremely snowbally. Build guide: [Defect Builds](/builds/builds-defect).

## Regent — the star-forging sovereign (new)

The Regent is arguably the strongest of the new characters, and the flashiest: a second resource pool called **Stars** that, unlike Energy, **carries over between turns**. Every turn is a conversion problem — turn Energy into Stars, Stars into damage, block, or draw.

The Regent has two main archetypes:

- **Star Engine** — generate more stars than you spend (Glow, Big Bang, Royal Gamble, Child of the Stars, Genesis) and spend them on finishers like Comet and Radiate. The consistent, high-Ascension pick.
- **Sovereign Blade** — Forge a signature sword all run and end bosses with one swing. Huge single-target damage, but less consistent and weaker against AoE fights.

Build guide: [Regent Builds](/builds/builds-regent).

## Necrobinder — the lich with a giant hand (new)

The Necrobinder fights as a **duo-unit**: a fragile spellcaster plus **Osty**, a massive reanimated skeletal hand. Her starting relic, Osty's Binding, summons it. Two archetypes:

- **Doom Deck** — mark enemies with Doom stacks; anyone at or below their mark is executed at the end of their turn. Higher card numbers, but you must survive until the clock ticks.
- **Osty Deck** — grow the hand with Summon effects and let it tank for you; Osty's health doesn't wear off like Block.

Build guide: [Necrobinder Builds](/builds/builds-overview-necrobinder).

## Which One Should You Start With?

- **First run:** Ironclad or Silent — the most forgiving power curves.
- **First 20 hours:** Defect, to learn the orb timing rhythm.
- **After that:** Regent for the highest ceiling, Necrobinder for the most unusual turns.
