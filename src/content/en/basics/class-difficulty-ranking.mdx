Which Slay the Spire 2 class should you play first? All five are viable — the community consensus ranks them by how quickly they forgive mistakes. Here is the difficulty ladder as of patch 0.105, with each class's real strengths and tax collectors.

## Difficulty ranking, easiest to hardest

1. **Ironclad** — ★☆☆☆☆
2. **Necrobinder** — ★★☆☆☆
3. **Regent** — ★★★☆☆
4. **Silent** — ★★★★☆
5. **Defect** — ★★★★★

## Ironclad — the friendly wall

**Strengths:** highest HP in the game (80), automatic post-combat healing via Burning Blood, fast-forming archetypes across armor, Strength and Vulnerable plans, balanced offense and defense.
**Weaknesses:** lower damage ceiling, less late-game burst, and a playstyle that can feel slow against fast enemy packs.

He wins by being hard to kill while every small advantage compounds. If you are learning the game's rhythms, this is where the rhythms are gentlest.

## Necrobinder — the forgiving summoner

**Strengths:** Osty absorbs hits meant for her fragile 66 HP; Doom-style damage plans come online early and grind steadily; simple to pilot, rarely explosive to lose with.
**Weaknesses:** slow early damage, low personal HP, and bosses that punish bloated decks.

The summoner looks complex and plays simple: keep the hand alive, let the debuff stack tick, survive.

## Regent — the planner's class

**Strengths:** Stars stack with no ceiling and pay out enormous late turns; the minion toolkit flexes into many builds.
**Weaknesses:** a slow opening act, and heavy demands on resource planning — unspent Stars are wasted Stars.

Ranked mid-tier not because he is weak but because his power arrives on schedule; players who can read two turns ahead will rank him higher every patch.

## Silent — the glass scalpel

**Strengths:** the highest burst ceiling in the roster, deep archetype variety across poison, daggers and discard, and the strongest card-draw engine in the game.
**Weaknesses:** 70 HP of pure glass; new players frequently die mid-Act 2 while the deck is still "one reward away."

She is ranked advanced because her margins are thin — the same decisions that make her untouchable in expert hands make her fragile in first-run ones.

## Defect — the mastery project

**Strengths:** the orb system has the highest ceiling in the game; a strong Defect is the best co-op support class in the party; late-run board clears are effortless.
**Weaknesses:** brutal energy management, unforgiving deckbuilding margins, and a weak opening act that can stall runs before they start.

Ranked hardest because it fails loudly: mismanaged slots and energy produce turns where the machine does nothing at all. Learn the game first, then come build the engine.

## The meta-note

This ranking reflects **Early Access patch 0.105** — balance changes move classes up and down between patches, and every class's real strength depends on the relics and cards a run actually offers. Treat the ladder as a starting order, not a verdict: pick Ironclad to learn, Necrobinder or Regent to relax, Silent and Defect to sweat.
