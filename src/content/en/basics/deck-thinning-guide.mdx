Every Slay the Spire 2 deck starts with the same problem: too many Strike-class basics and not enough of the cards you actually picked. **Deck thinning** — removing and transforming cards — is the quiet stat boost almost every player underbuys. Here is how to do it efficiently, and when to stop.

## The one removal route you can plan around: shops

Event removals are luck. Shop removals are not — a card removal service appears in shops reliably, which makes it **the only removal source you can budget for across a whole run**.

So the spending rule is simple: **when a shop offers removal, that is the first place your gold goes** — before potions, before the shiny rare card. Removing a basic Strike is often a bigger power spike than buying a mid-tier attack, because every draw after it is 2-3% more likely to be a card that wins fights.

## Where the rest of the removal comes from

- **Question-mark events.** Several events remove a card for gold or for free. The Act 1 beggar-style removal for 75 gold is close to an auto-pick; see our [Act 1 events guide](/basics/act-1-events-guide).
- **Transform effects.** Items and events that change a card into a random new one are removal with a lottery ticket attached — grab them when the replaced card is a basic.
- **Route planning.** When the map offers a question-mark-heavy path, and your deck still carries dead weight, take it.

## How big should a deck be?

Aim for **15-20 cards**. That is not a fetish — it is arithmetic. Every removed card raises the average quality of your draws; past that point, removals stop paying unless you are building toward a specific engine.

## The infinite combo trap

Many players assume winning requires an **infinite loop** — draw ≥ the loop's card count, energy recovery ≥ its total cost, damage ≥ the kill threshold, all at once. It is true that infinites demand aggressive thinning and transforming. It is equally true that forcing an infinite without the removal to support it produces a bloated, stuttering deck that loops nothing and dies to Act 2 elites.

The honest decision rule:

- **Generous removal luck** (multiple event removals, early shop access)? Push toward the infinite.
- **Normal removal luck?** Build a conventional value engine — consistent draw, steady block, reliable damage. A 20-card deck that cycles predictably clears the spire just as dead.

## When you can't thin: increase density

If removal options were scarce, the alternative to thinning is **densifying** — stop adding cards entirely, take upgrades over new cards, and prefer picks that duplicate your core cards' roles. A run with 18 cards and six of them doing the real work plays nearly as clean as a thinned deck.
