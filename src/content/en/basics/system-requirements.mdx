Slay the Spire 2 is one of the least demanding games on Steam — and that is official, not speculation. The game runs on a 64-bit OS with a dual-core processor, 4 GB of RAM and a 1 GB graphics card, and it ships with full Windows, macOS and Linux support. If your machine has launched anything in the last decade, you can climb the spire.

## Windows requirements (official)

**Minimum:**

- **OS:** Windows 10 (64-bit) with a 64-bit processor
- **CPU:** Dual-core 2.0 GHz
- **Memory:** 4 GB RAM
- **GPU:** DirectX 12 or Vulkan compatible, 1 GB VRAM
- **Storage:** 4 GB available space

**Recommended:** the same baseline — Mega Crit's official recommended spec targets modern integrated graphics. This is a deckbuilder, not a ray-tracing showcase; the game is deliberately built to run everywhere.

## macOS requirements (official)

- **OS:** macOS 12.0 (Monterey) or newer
- **CPU:** Intel Core i5 (dual-core)
- **Memory:** 4 GB RAM
- **GPU:** Metal-compatible GPU with 1 GB VRAM
- **Storage:** 4 GB available space

## Linux / Steam Deck requirements (official)

- **OS:** Ubuntu 20.04 LTS, SteamOS 3.0 or newer
- **CPU:** Dual-core x86_64 at 2.0 GHz
- **Memory:** 4 GB RAM
- **GPU:** Vulkan 1.0 or OpenGL 3.x compatible
- **Storage:** 4 GB available space

With native Vulkan support and a 4 GB install, the game is a natural fit for the Steam Deck and older Linux handhelds — no Proton workarounds required.

## What this means in practice

The sequel adds co-op multiplayer and far more cards, relics and effects than the original, yet the official spec barely moved: any mainstream desktop or laptop from the last ten years runs it at full settings. If you are upgrading a machine specifically for this game, don't — put that budget toward the [co-op party](/basics/how-to-join-multiplayer) you are about to invite instead.
