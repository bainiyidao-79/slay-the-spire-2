**Tezcatara's Pumpkin Candle** was always the strongest energy relic for two acts and a ghost in the third. The 0.105 rework rebuilt its limit — and quietly turned it into one of the best Ancient relics in the game.

<div class="art-ph lead"><img src="/images/sts2-event-blessing.webp" loading="eager" alt="An Ancient's gift in Slay the Spire 2"><figcaption>One extra energy a day. The candle just needed a match.</figcaption></div>

## Old versus new

- **Old:** gain 1 energy at the start of each turn. Extinguishes at the start of **Act 3** — permanently.
- **New (0.105):** gain 1 energy at the start of each turn. Extinguishes **after 5 combats** — and **can be re-Kindled at any rest site**, resetting the 5-combat count.

The change replaces a cliff (dead the moment Act 3 begins, precisely when you need energy most) with a cycle: five fights of +1 energy, then a rest site re-lights it.

## The value math

- **Baseline:** 5 combats × roughly 10 turns = 50+ bonus energy per lighting cycle, from a relic that occupies no card slots and asks nothing of your deck.
- **With re-Kindling:** an act with 2–3 rest sites sustains the candle across essentially the whole run. The energy tax moves to route planning, not to the relic.
- **It stacks** with other energy effects — extra energy relics and potions simply multiply the tempo.

## Timing and route notes

1. **Take it early** — the earlier the pickup, the more cycles the candle funds.
2. **Route rest sites as candle maintenance:** a path that touches a campfire every five fights keeps the flame permanent. The [map routing guide](/basics/map-room-types-route-planning) already told you to do this; now it pays energy.
3. **Spend the powered turns on setup:** the extra energy is worth most on the turns you play a scaling Power plus a full hand — not on chump blocks.

The rework changed the relic from "great, then gone" to "great, then go find a match." For energy-hungry engines — Regent star banking, Defect orb setups, any high-cost curve — it is now a run-long companion rather than a two-act rental.
