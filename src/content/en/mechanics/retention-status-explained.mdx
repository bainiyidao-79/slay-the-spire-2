The **retention** effect answers the hand-management player's prayer: *"At the end of your turn, you may retain X card(s)"* — where X is the number of stacks. Instead of discarding your whole hand, you choose that many cards to carry into the next turn.

## How it works

- **Trigger timing:** end of turn, during the cleanup step — before normal discarding happens.
- **You choose:** retention is a player pick, not an auto-keep. Select up to X cards; everything else discards as usual.
- **Kept cards stay in hand:** they carry over and are live on your next turn — not exiled, not delayed.
- **Stackable:** each additional stack raises how many cards you can carry.
- **It arrives as a Power:** cast it once and the effect persists for the fight.

## What it enables

Retention is the glue for three classic plans:

1. **Setup pieces** — combo starters and scaling Powers that you drew a turn too early simply wait, instead of costing a second draw.
2. **Retain synergies** — cards with the Retain keyword (they normally sit in hand forever) and payoffs that reward a fat hand both appreciate the extra carry capacity.
3. **Boss insurance** — holding a burst card or a defensive answer across the turn boundary means the boss's telegraphed big attack meets your prepared answer, not your hope.

## Decks that want it

Slow-scaling decks — Regent star banking, Defect orb setups, Necrobinder summon growth — value retention highest, because their key cards are worth more held than discarded. Aggressive shiv and Claw decks, which refill constantly, barely notice it. For the underlying hand economy, see [the four core mechanics](/basics/four-core-mechanics).
