The number that put the launch in the record books: **526,362 concurrent players** — Slay the Spire 2's Steam concurrency blowing past half a million, **nearly ten times the original game's all-time record of 57,025**.

SteamDB's chart made the generational comparison visually absurd: a genre that the first game effectively invented in 2019, peaking in the tens of thousands across its whole life, now running at half a million simultaneous climbers in its launch weekend. The sequel hadn't just matched its predecessor's success; it had re-based the entire scale.

The milestone landed on the same weekend as the rest of the launch story — [95% positive reviews](/news/launch-day-95-percent-positive), the [global #1 seller slot](/news/tops-steam-global-sellers), and a player-base celebration the developers called overwhelming. The roguelike category's concurrent record, previously contested by full-priced blockbusters, now belonged to a $30 Early Access deckbuilding game.

And the graph still wasn't done: the Sunday push carried the peak to [570,000-plus](/news/concurrent-570k-2026-record), top-twenty in Steam's all-time list — a number that stopped being a card-game story and became a platform story.
