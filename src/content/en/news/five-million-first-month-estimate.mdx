The third-party accountants weighed in, and their number out-grew the official one: analytics firm **Alinea Analytics** estimates Slay the Spire 2 sold roughly **5.3 million copies in its first month** — with about **$108 million** of Steam revenue in March alone.

The estimate came from analyst Reece Elliott, and it reconciles with the official beats that preceded it: [3 million copies in week one](/news/three-million-first-week-roadmap) per Mega Crit, meaning the back three weeks of March added another two-plus million on the chart of a month where, per Alinea, only Pearl Abyss's **Red Desert** sold more.

The $108 million March figure is the one that resets expectations for indie-scale development: a $30 Early Access title, no marketing machine, [no microtransactions](/news/no-microtransactions-pledge) — and a monthly gross that most live-service games spend years chasing.

The estimate also retroactively explained the launch's chaos: the [server-crashing crowds](/news/launch-crashes-steam-servers), the [570K concurrency record](/news/concurrent-570k-2026-record), and the [weekly chart #1](/news/tops-weekly-steam-seller-chart) were all early readings of a demand curve that stayed steep through the month.

For the genre, Alinea's number is the headline: the deckbuilding-roguelike audience the original created is now demonstrably one of PC gaming's largest commercial communities — and the sequel collected its first month's tribute accordingly.
