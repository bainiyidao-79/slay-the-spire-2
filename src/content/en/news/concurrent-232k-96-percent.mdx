The launch-day counter kept rolling: by SteamDB's evening snapshot, **Slay the Spire 2 had passed 232,000 concurrent players** — roughly **four times the original Slay the Spire's all-time peak** of about 57,000, a record the first game had set only months earlier.

The quality metrics climbed with the crowd: nearly **3,600 reviews at 96% positive**, signaling that the influx wasn't curiosity traffic — the people arriving were staying and liking what they found. Commentary at the time noted 300,000 looked reachable before midnight; it was, and the graph kept going.

For the original's community, the four-times framing was the story. The first Slay the Spire was a slow-burn classic that built its audience over years; the sequel opened at a multiple of its ceiling within hours, on the strength of a seven-year wait, two beloved new classes, and the series' first [four-player co-op](/basics/how-to-join-multiplayer).

The milestones that followed read like a countdown in reverse: [280,000](/news/concurrent-280k-record), then [410,000](/news/concurrent-410k-vs-maratthon) past a $250 million blockbuster's numbers, then [500,000](/news/concurrent-500k-ten-times-sequel) — ten times the original — and finally [570,000-plus](/news/concurrent-570k-2026-record), top-twenty in Steam's all-time list.
