The balance patch story escalated on March 22: the v0.100.0 beta's sweeping nerfs to **infinite-combo playstyles** — the community's most polished endgame strategies — triggered a one-day flood of nearly **10,000 negative Steam reviews**, overwhelmingly from Chinese players, dragging the **Simplified Chinese rating down to 56%** while other language regions held their "Overwhelmingly Positive" status.

The context that made the reaction intense rather than petulant:

- **Infinite combos are an endgame institution.** In the original spire, assembling an infinite loop was the community's crown achievement — a skill expression, not an exploit. Nerfing the foundation of that tradition in week three read, to hardcore players, as removing the game's ceiling.
- **The timing collided with confidence.** The game had just sold **4.6 million copies** — with roughly a third from Chinese regions. The audience funding the record launch felt its preferred playstyle was the first thing on the chopping block.
- **The beta/branch structure confused expectations.** Changes flagged as beta testing still read, to most players, as the game's direction.

The episode's resolution became its own story — and a case study in community management: a [direct official response](/news/official-response-china-feedback) addressing Chinese players' concerns specifically, followed by the [0.101 patch](/news/0-101-patch-reverts-nerfs) that rolled back the most-criticized nerfs and publicly credited player feedback for the reversal.
