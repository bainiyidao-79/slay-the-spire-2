The balance-storm arc closed the way community managers dream about: with a rollback patch, a public thank-you, and a player credit in the patch notes. **0.101.0** hit the beta branch on March 27, reversing the most-hated changes from the [March 20 balance pass](/news/beta-patch-backlash-march) and fixing a stack of bugs along the way.

The human moment came from developer **Anthony Giovannetti**, who singled out the reversal of the Silent card **Prepared** ("act early / act soon" in CN coverage) and thanked "a certain Chinese player's feedback submitted through F2" — the player had argued the card was *integral to the Silent's identity* rather than merely strong, and that argument carried the day. A balance dispute that had fueled [thousands of negative reviews](/news/infinite-nerf-china-review-bomb) ended with a named developer crediting a named feedback channel.

The patch's broader significance:

- **The rollback precedent.** Mega Crit demonstrated that beta-branch nerfs are proposals, not verdicts — a stance that reframes every future balance fight.
- **The channel vindication.** The F2 in-game feedback tool, promoted in the [official response](/news/official-response-china-feedback) as the most valuable input, was now proven to move patches.
- **The trust repair.** The Simplified Chinese rating dip that started the storm recovered as the community registered that outrage, channeled through the right pipe, actually worked.

For players, the practical takeaway doubled as design philosophy: complain through the channel that reads, argue identity over power, and the spire's balance pendulum can swing back.
