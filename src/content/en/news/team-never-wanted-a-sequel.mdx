The counterintuitive origin story, from Mega Crit co-founder **Casey Yano**: Slay the Spire 2 was never the plan. The original intent was to keep expanding the first game indefinitely — a live-update model — and let the spire grow forever.

Three forces made that untenable:

- **Technical ceilings.** The original's codebase hit hard limits as content expanded; the old foundation couldn't carry the features the team wanted next.
- **Multi-platform strain.** Supporting consoles and mobile on the aging stack added compounding overhead — effort spent porting rather than building.
- **The Humble Games shutdown.** The studio's publishing partner effectively closed in 2024, stalling the infrastructure that ongoing updates depended on.

With the update path blocked on three sides, the sequel stopped being a choice and became the necessary vessel for everything the team still wanted to make — which reframes the [Godot engine switch](/news/slay-the-spire-2-switches-to-godot-engine) as part of the same decision: if you're forced to rebuild, rebuild on a foundation you own.

The story adds a wry footnote to the launch records: the [570K concurrency peak](/news/concurrent-570k-2026-record), the [three-million first week](/news/three-million-first-week-roadmap), the [9/10 review](/news/ign-nine-review) — all of it traces back to a forced migration the team initially resisted. The reluctant sequel is, by every measurable outcome, the best thing the studio ever did. Sometimes the coin flip and the closed doors point the same way.
