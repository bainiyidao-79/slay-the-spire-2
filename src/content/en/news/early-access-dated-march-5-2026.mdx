February 20, 2026: the wait got a date. Mega Crit confirmed **Slay the Spire 2's Early Access launch for March 5, 2026** — weeks after the [delay announcement](/news/early-access-delayed-to-march-2026) set the window.

The announcement packaged the sequel's headline features into one message:

- **A thousand-year sleep ends.** The spire reopens "hungrier and more dangerous than ever," with new cards, events, environments, enemies and story chapters across the climb.
- **4-player online co-op** — the series-first, letting parties climb one shared map with separate decks and scaled enemies. (The practical version: see our [multiplayer guide](/basics/how-to-join-multiplayer).)
- **Day-one Simplified Chinese**, a nod to the original game's enormous Chinese-speaking player base — a detail that would matter enormously in the [launch-week reviews](/news/launch-day-95-percent-positive) and the [beta-balance drama](/news/infinite-nerf-china-review-bomb) that followed.

The marketing framing leaned on legacy: the original "practically defined the modern deckbuilding roguelike," and the sequel's job was to climb beside it, not replace it — sharper strategies, newer horrors, same one-more-run pull.

With the date public, the community countdown began in earnest: the [China price confirmation](/news/china-price-88-yuan) two weeks later was the last pre-launch domino before the spire opened and [Steam's servers buckled](/news/launch-crashes-steam-servers) under the rush.
