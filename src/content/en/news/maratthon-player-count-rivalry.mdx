Launch day handed the internet a gift-wrapped rivalry: Slay the Spire 2 and Bungie's extraction shooter **Marathon** shipped the same day — a tiny indie card roguelike against a AAA live-service shooter — and the player-count comparison wrote itself into every timeline.

The numbers were lopsided fast. The spire's concurrency blew past **200,000 and kept climbing**; Marathon's Steam numbers struggled to clear six figures, well below its playtest peaks. Social media did what social media does, and Mega Crit's own account leaned in — posting a tongue-in-cheek "congratulations to the Marathon team on the launch" flavored with a jab about being out-numbers-ed by a small indie passion project.

The internet received it exactly as badly as it read. What the team intended as locker-room banter landed as kicking a struggling peer on its launch day, and the blowback came fast — including from voices who noted that Marathon's team had its own bruising launch week without needing a second one.

Three days later, Mega Crit co-founder **Casey Yano** owned it: the congratulation post "read as far meaner than intended," he said — the team hadn't expected the player-count comparison to swing as hard as it did, and the joke had aged into something else entirely. The [apology](/news/bungie-joke-apology) was accepted about as gracefully as internet apologies get, and the episode settled into launch-week lore: the day the card game out-drew the shooter, and the winner learned to gloat more carefully.
