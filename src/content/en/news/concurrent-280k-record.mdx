Seven years after the original defined the genre, its sequel set the genre's new ceiling: **Slay the Spire 2's Steam concurrency passed 280,000** on launch day — with commentators openly forecasting 300,000 before the first weekend ended.

The frame around the number was legacy. The original Slay the Spire, released in 2017, remains the genre's founding classic — its depth and balance still the industry's reference standard. That pedigree made the sequel's trajectory less a surprise than a coronation: the audience was always there, dormant, waiting for a reason to climb again.

The launch context did the rest — [95% positive reviews](/news/launch-day-95-percent-positive) validating the wait, a [top-of-chart seller debut](/news/tops-steam-global-sellers), launch traffic heavy enough to [wobble Steam's own infrastructure](/news/launch-crashes-steam-servers), and a concurrency graph that had already doubled twice in a day: [189K](/news/launch-concurrent-190k) at dawn, [232K](/news/concurrent-232k-96-percent) by evening, 280K as the weekend opened.

The forecasters were right, and then some. The weekend pushed the peak past 410,000 — out-leaping a certain $250 million shooter's launch — and the record run continued to 570,000-plus, a figure that put a small-team card game in Steam's all-time top twenty.
