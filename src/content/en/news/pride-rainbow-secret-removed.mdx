The community's code-divers struck again in late May: hidden inside Slay the Spire 2's files, players surfaced a **pride-rainbow menu easter egg** — triggerable by setting the device clock to **June 25**, Pride month's shadow date, which swapped the game's menu into a rainbow-themed celebration screen.

The trail, as the discovery coverage pieced together: the first clue came from a player known as Madeline's creation thread, and deeper source-digging confirmed the timing-gated rainbow content was real and functional. The codicil: the **1.06 patch quietly removed** both the code path and the screen, and the easter egg ceased to exist.

The discovery lit up the discourse for a few days, along lines regular followers of the game's [May rating turbulence](/news/may-review-bomb-wave) could predict — the debate centering less on the easter egg's existence than on its quiet removal: whether the studio's earlier public statements and the code's presence had been in tension, and what "removing a celebration" communicates versus never shipping it at all.

What's verifiable beyond argument: the easter egg was real, date-gated, and is gone as of 1.06. Whether June's menu ever glows again is now entirely in Mega Crit's hands — and given this studio's track record of [answering feedback through patches](/news/official-response-china-feedback), the response, whatever it is, will arrive in code.
