Early Access's roadmap got its first real substance in April: Mega Crit co-founder **Casey Yano** confirmed that **three brand-new modes** are in active development — each aimed at a different slice of the player base that the launch's record numbers revealed.

The three tracks, as described:

- **Competitive mode** — leaderboards and/or player-versus-player elements, giving the spire's theorycrafters a scoreboard to fight over rather than just Discord consensus.
- **Quick-play mode** — a tightened, shorter-session format that preserves the core deckbuilding depth while respecting players who can't commit to a full climb.
- **Social co-op** — an expansion of multiplayer beyond the current four-player climb, exploring new synchronous and asynchronous ways to play together.

The reveal's context was the numbers: a launch that set Steam concurrency records and needed [three million sales in week one](/news/three-million-first-week-roadmap) just to contain its own lobby queues. A bigger player base fragments into more play patterns — ladder climbers, lunch-break roguelikers, friend groups — and the three-mode plan is the studio's answer to that fragmentation rather than a pivot away from the core.

Yano coupled the news with a reiteration of the Early Access values: the base game's integrity stays put — [no microtransactions](/news/no-microtransactions-pledge), content delivered through updates — while the mode experiments expand what "one more run" can mean.

No dates were attached. The safe read: modes ship as they mature, in the same patch cadence that's already delivered [balance rollbacks](/news/0-101-patch-reverts-nerfs) and content drops.
