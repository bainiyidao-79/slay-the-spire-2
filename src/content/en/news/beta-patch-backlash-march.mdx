Every Early Access balance patch is a negotiation with the community. Slay the Spire 2's March 20 beta update lost the room: the balance pass drew broad player anger across community platforms, summarized in the moment's most-quoted grievance — *"if you can't balance it, don't touch it."*

The complaint stack, as the roundup coverage captured it:

- **Nerfs that broke ordinary decks.** The charge wasn't that strong strategies were nerfed — it was that adjustments to staples like the pack-hoarding and draw packages disrupted *normal, non-infinite* decks that were never the target.
- **Infinite combos nerfed early.** Players felt the infinite-combo ecosystem was still in its discovery phase — cutting it prematurely punished experimentation for its own sake.
- **Problem enemies untouched.** The loudest single note: widely-hated enemy designs came through the balance pass unscathed while player tools got shaved.
- **Tone-deaf sequencing.** A game [fresh off a 570K-record launch](/news/concurrent-570k-2026-record) changing foundational rules felt, to critics, like the studio prioritizing data over the live experience.

The episode's epilogue is what made it a story rather than a mood: the feedback loop worked. Within days the conversation had the developers' attention — culminating in the [official response on feedback channels](/news/official-response-china-feedback) and, a week later, the [0.101 patch](/news/0-101-patch-reverts-nerfs) that rolled back the most-hated changes and thanked players by name.
