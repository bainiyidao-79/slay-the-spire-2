The last pre-launch domino fell on February 28: **Slay the Spire 2's Chinese region price was confirmed at 88 yuan** — exactly matching the original game's price point, a deliberate continuity signal to the series' largest regional fanbase.

The announcement rounded out the launch picture for Chinese players: the sequel would go live in the early hours of March 6 local time (2:00 AM Beijing), rebuilt on the new engine for better visuals and expanded modding flexibility, with the summon-flavored [Necrobinder](/classes/necrobinder-character-guide), the Doom mechanic, and the deckbuilding-roguelike loop that made the original a genre founder.

The pricing mattered more than it looks. The original Slay the Spire's Chinese-speaking community is enormous and famously vocal — a fact that would shape the sequel's first months in both directions: day-one review floods in [launch week](/news/launch-day-95-percent-positive), and later the [beta-balance feedback wave](/news/infinite-nerf-china-review-bomb) that Mega Crit would publicly address. Matching the original's price read as the studio planting its flag: same value, same audience, same respect.

There was also a bundle for newcomers: the **Slay the Spire Collection** packaging both games at a steep combined discount.
