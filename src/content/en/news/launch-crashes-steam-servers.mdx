The demand outgrew the plumbing. On launch day, players reporting checkout failures, library hiccups and store-page timeouts led Kotaku to the story of the day: **Slay the Spire 2's opening rush had crashed parts of Steam** — the second time in recent memory a single game's launch had done it, after Hollow Knight: Silksong's release.

The comparison was flattering company. Steam outages on launch day are the purest demand signal the platform produces: not a marketing claim, but infrastructure buckling under the number of people trying to give a company money at the same time. The spire's reopen — seven years after the original, paid Early Access, no pre-launch free-to-play hype engine — drew a crowd Steam's systems didn't expect for a card game.

The crash itself was brief; Steam's services recovered while the launch window was still hot, and the players who got through found the [reviews flooding in positive](/news/launch-day-95-percent-positive) and the [concurrency graph climbing](/news/launch-concurrent-190k) toward numbers no deckbuilder had touched.

It set the tone for the week that followed: a [global #1 seller slot](/news/tops-steam-global-sellers), a headline rivalry with a $250 million shooter's launch numbers, and a first-week [three-million sales report](/news/three-million-first-week-roadmap) from a studio that once chose its next project by [flipping a coin](/news/coin-flip-origin-story).
