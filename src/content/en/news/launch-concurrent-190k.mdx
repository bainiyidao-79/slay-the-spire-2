The launch-day reviews were one story; the player counts were another. By SteamDB's tracking, **Slay the Spire 2 hit a peak of 189,028 concurrent players** on launch day itself — before the first weekend, before the streamers' evening blocks, before the word of mouth really started.

The context made the number louder. This was an Early Access deckbuilder — a genre where "concurrent player" counts are typically five digits and celebrated. The sequel opened at six figures in an afternoon, riding the [95% positive review wave](/news/launch-day-95-percent-positive) and a launch so crowded it [briefly strained Steam's own infrastructure](/news/launch-crashes-steam-servers).

And the trajectory was the story: launch-day numbers are usually the peak. Slay the Spire 2's were the floor. Within 24 hours the peak pushed past **230,000**, within a week it crossed **500,000**, and by the first Sunday it stood at **570,000-plus** — roughly **ten times the original game's all-time record**. (The full climb, step by step: [232K](/news/concurrent-232k-96-percent) → [280K](/news/concurrent-280k-record) → [410K](/news/concurrent-410k-vs-maratthon) → [500K](/news/concurrent-500k-ten-times-sequel) → [570K](/news/concurrent-570k-2026-record).)

For a seven-year-gone franchise returning as a paid Early Access title, the day-one number was the market's first answer to a simple question: did anyone still want to climb the spire? The answer was 189,000 people — and then the weekend started.
