May brought the sequel's second rating storm, and this one wasn't about balance. Criticism flared over **consultant credits** in the game's staff list — most prominently the inclusion of media critic Anita Sarkeesian, a figure long controversial in gaming spaces — and the resulting review wave pushed Slay the Spire 2's **Simplified Chinese Steam rating down to "Mostly Negative."**

The notable shift was geographic. Previous review waves had been predominantly Chinese-community affairs — the [March infinite-nerf backlash](/news/infinite-nerf-china-review-bomb) — with English-language regions standing apart. This round crossed over: players who had earlier dismissed the Chinese reviews as oversensitivity now posted critical reviews of their own over the credits issue, and the English-region rating sagged toward the same territory.

The compounding effect was cumulative fatigue. The game had absorbed the [March balance storm](/news/beta-patch-backlash-march), recovered through the [0.101 rollback](/news/0-101-patch-reverts-nerfs), and now faced a culture-war grievance that no patch could mechanically fix — consultant credits aren't a tunable value, and Mega Crit's playbook of data-plus-dialogue had no lever for it.

The episode sits in the longer arc that Early Access games increasingly navigate: a record-breaking [launch](/news/launch-day-95-percent-positive) builds a playerbase of millions, and a community that large will litigate everything — balance, credits, values — in public, on the rating page. The [subsequent May review wave](/news/may-review-bomb-wave) kept the pressure on before the curve finally bent back.
