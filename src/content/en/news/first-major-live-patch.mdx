A month into Early Access, the first major patch hit the **main branch** — consolidating everything the three prior beta branches had been testing into the game everyone plays. The headline changes touch the run's economy at both ends: how hard high-Ascension climbs punish you, and what the shop charges for its most important service.

## Ascension 6, redesigned

The old Ascension 6 modifier removed rest sites from the map — a flat, bludgeoning tax on climbs. The rework swaps the mechanism entirely: **shop card-removal prices go up**, from 75 gold per removal to 100 (and the scaling increase rises from 25 to 50 per use). Same design goal — high Ascension should starve your deck-thinning budget — but now it's a money decision you plan around rather than a map feature you simply lack. (Why removal matters this much: see the [deck-thinning guide](/basics/deck-thinning-guide).)

## Shop economy, rebalanced

- **All shop relics cost 25 gold less.**
- Shops **no longer stock gold-generating relics** — closing the loop where a run could buy its way into infinite money.

The pairing shifts shop identity: relics are cheaper because gold is tighter, and the "buy the money printer" degenerate line is gone.

## Map generation stabilized

The patch also reworked map generation for consistency — fewer wild outliers in map structure, more predictable act shapes, which quietly matters for every [route-planning decision](/basics/map-room-types-route-planning) in the game.

The patch's meta-message matched its content: beta experiments that survived contact with players graduated; the ones that didn't ([see the March rollback](/news/0-101-patch-reverts-nerfs)) were reworked before ever reaching the main branch. The Early Access pipeline is working as advertised.
