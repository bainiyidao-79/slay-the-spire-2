The launch weekend turned a head-to-head comparison into a parable. **Slay the Spire 2 peaked above 410,000 concurrent players** on Steam — while **Marathon**, the same-day release backed by a reported **$250 million** budget, topped out around **88,000**. Indie card game versus AAA shooter; no contest.

The internet's favorite kind of numbers fight, in other words — and Mega Crit, riding the biggest launch in its history, leaned into it with a cheeky social-media post playing up the David-versus-Goliath ledger.

The saga had one more beat — the [apology](/news/bungie-joke-apology) after the gloating read meaner than intended — but the underlying data never got walked back, because it didn't need to be:

- **410K vs 88K** on the same launch weekend is a ten-year development story compressed into one chart.
- The original Slay the Spire's formula — $30, no marketing machine, pure word of mouth — scaled rather than faded in its sequel.
- And the spire's numbers kept climbing after the blockbusters' usual launch decay: [500K](/news/concurrent-500k-ten-times-sequel) the next day, [570K](/news/concurrent-570k-2026-record) by Sunday.

For the deckbuilding genre at large, the weekend was a statement of market size: the audience that made Slay the Spire a classic hadn't just persisted for seven years — it had multiplied, and it showed up for the sequel in numbers the genre had never seen.
