Slay The Spire 2 Bosses & How to Beat Them

All Slay the Spire 2 bosses by act: where each boss appears, boss rewards, and full Act 3 guides for Queen, Test Subject and Aeonglass with attack patterns.

There are nine Slay the Spire 2 bosses across three acts: six Act 1 bosses (three per map), three Act 2 bosses, and three Act 3 bosses — Queen & Torch Head Amalgam, Test Subject, and Aeonglass. Every act ends with one of them at the top of the Spire map, and Act 3's fights are the most complex in the game, built around multi-phase patterns. This guide covers where each boss appears, what you get for beating them, and full breakdowns of the Act 3 bosses whose attack patterns are documented.

How Boss Fights Work in Slay the Spire 2

Bosses are the most difficult enemies in Slay the Spire 2. Compared to normal enemies and elites, they have significantly more health, more powerful move sets, and special gimmicks that either cripple you or heavily buff the boss. Every boss has a unique portrait shown at the top of the Spire map — the first thing you should do when entering an act is check which boss you're up against, because several of them hard-counter specific deck types.

The map node directly before a boss fight is always a Rest Site, so you can heal before the fight. The one exception is the double boss fight at the end of Act 3 on Ascension 10, where the second boss is fought immediately after the first with no break in between.

Boss rewards: Beating the Act 1 or Act 2 boss gives you 100 Gold (75 on Ascension 3 and higher), a choice between three Rare cards, and sometimes a random Potion. You're then sent to the start of the next act, where an Ancient heals you for 100% of your missing health (80% on Ascension 2 and higher) and offers a choice between three of their unique Ancient Relics.

Where bosses appear (regular singleplayer runs):

Act Boss floor Bosses
Act 1 Floor 17 6 bosses, split by map (see below)
Act 2 Floor 33 The Insatiable, Knowledge Demon, Kaiser Crab
Act 3 Floor 48 Queen & Torch Head Amalgam, Test Subject, Aeonglass

Floor numbers shift in a few situations. If you pick up the Golden Compass at the start of Act 2, the map becomes a single-path map that is two floors longer, so every boss floor after that increases by 2. In multiplayer, each act is one floor shorter, putting the Act 1 boss on floor 16, the Act 2 boss on floor 31, and the Act 3 boss on floor 45. On Ascension 10, a second Act 3 boss — always different from the first — waits on floor 49, and it's visible on the map from the start next to the first boss's icon.

Act 1 Bosses (Floor 17)

Act 1 has two maps, and each carries its own pool of three possible bosses:

Map Possible bosses
Overgrowth Ceremonial Beast, The Kin, Vantom
Underdocks Waterfall Giant, Soul Fysh, Lagavulin Matriarch

One of these six appears on floor 17 of every run. Detailed attack patterns for the Act 1 roster are not documented in our sources yet — treat specific openings and phases as to be confirmed. One widely-cited read from high-level play: Vantom is considered the hardest Act 1 boss, requiring nine hits before you can start dealing real damage to it, plus a lot of block. Checking the boss portrait at the top of the map before you path through Act 1 tells you which of the six you're preparing for.

Act 2 Bosses (Floor 33)

Act 2's boss pool is three bosses: The Insatiable, Knowledge Demon, and Kaiser Crab. One of them appears on floor 33 of every run.

As with Act 1, individual attack patterns for these three bosses are not documented in our sources yet — to be confirmed. What the sources do establish is that Act 2 is where your deck must start scaling: the Act 2 elites alone hit for around 30 and sit near 200 health, so arriving at the Act 2 boss with no scaling plan is the most common reason runs end. If you keep losing at or before this boss, the fix is usually in your Act 1 pathing and card picks rather than the fight itself.

Act 3 Bosses (Floor 48, and 49 on Ascension 10)

Act 3's bosses are more mechanically complex than anything in Act 2, with multi-phase fights where the boss's abilities change over time. If your run has gone well, these fights can end up easier than the Act 2 bosses — your deck should be close to finished by now.

Queen & Torch Head Amalgam

You fight the Queen and her minion, the Torch Head Amalgam, at the same time, which gives you two options: kill the minion first and then take out the Queen, or focus the Queen down while tanking the minion's attacks. Either works, but focusing the Queen has produced the best results when your deck has enough block to absorb the Amalgam's hits.

  • Turn 1 — Chains of Binding: the Queen makes the first three cards you draw Bound, meaning you can only play one of those three per turn. Cards after the first three aren't affected. This is an exceptionally nasty debuff, and it's the main reason focusing fire on the Queen is the recommended strategy. (Effects like Hellraiser's are a way around it.)
  • Queen's pattern: Turn 1 Chains of Binding → Turn 2 Strategic (inflicts 99 Frail, Weak, and Vulnerable) → Turn 3 Empower (strength on the minion) + Defense → after that she keeps buffing the minion and blocking.
  • Torch Head Amalgam's pattern: Turn 1 Attack → Turn 2 Attack → Turn 3 Attack x3 → repeats.
  • After the minion dies, the Queen switches to: Turn 1 Empower (buffs herself) → Turn 2 Attack x5 → Turn 3 Attack → repeats.

If you'd rather kill the Amalgam first, it has a relatively small health pool and shouldn't be too hard to remove — but be aware it can grow extremely powerful while you chew through the Queen.

Test Subject

Test Subject is a three-phase fight. Each time you kill the boss it becomes invulnerable, heals, gains health, and changes its abilities — you have to kill it three times to end the fight. (Its exact move set is still being mapped by the community, so treat the details below as a reliable guideline rather than gospel.)

  • Phase 1 (Subject C#18): the least health of the three phases, so kill it quickly. The boss has Enrage, gaining Strength every time you play a Skill — brutal for skill-heavy decks, e.g. a Necrobinder deck full of Souls. Pattern: Turn 1 Attack → Turn 2 Attack + Strategic (Vulnerable) → repeats. The Enrage Strength clears when the phase ends.
  • Phase 2: the buff changes — unblocked damage the boss deals inflicts Wounds into your deck. Play more cautiously here, because Wounds stay in your deck for the final phase. Pattern: Turn 1 Attack x3 → Turn 2 Attack → Turn 3 Attack x4 → repeats.
  • Phase 3: the boss gains Intangible every other turn, reducing the damage of any hit against it to 1. Set up big hands for the turns it's vulnerable, or use multi-hit attacks to chip through. Be especially careful on turn 4 of the cycle, with multi-hit attacks back to back. Pattern: Turn 1 Attack x3 → Turn 2 Attack → Turn 3 Strategic (adds 3 Burns to your deck) + Empower → Turn 4 Attack x3 → repeats.

Aeonglass

Aeonglass is the third Act 3 boss, added in Early Access update v0.107.1 when it replaced the Doormaker. Its attack patterns aren't documented in our sources yet — to be confirmed.

Legacy: The Door & The Doormaker (pre-v0.107.1)

The Doormaker was the original Act 3 boss in the first Early Access version, and it's still worth knowing because it shows the fight design and still exists on the Beta branch in a different form.

The fight starts against the Door — only 155 HP, with strong but manageable attacks (typically Turn 1 Attack → Turn 2 Attack x2 for 15 each → Turn 3 Attack + Empower, sometimes just three straight attacks). Killing the Door is not the end: the Doormaker appears behind it, stunned for his first turn, then hits with extremely strong attacks before transforming back into the Door after three turns. The critical detail: the Door respawns at full health each cycle, but the Doormaker never heals, so with strong block the fight grinds him down. Save spare energy at low Door health — there's no break between the Door "dying" and the Doormaker appearing, and free damage into the stunned Doormaker makes the fight much easier. His pattern: Turn 1 Stunned → Turn 2 Attack → Turn 3 a huge attack + Empower + Escape (back to Door). Weathering that turn-3 attack is the hardest part of the fight.

On the Beta branch, the fight changes completely: the Door has infinite HP and summons the Doormaker, who then cycles three oppressive statuses — Hunger (your attacks and skills Exhaust after playing, and he attacks for 12 x2), Scrutiny (no card drawing, and a 30-damage attack is primed), and Grasp (playing any card costs an extra energy, plus a 20-damage attack). It's a marathon, not a sprint — use the summoning turn to buff up and prepare, then pick your moments.

Preparing for Act 3 Bosses

By Act 3, the fight is usually decided by decisions from Acts 1 and 2 rather than the boss itself. The high-level checklist: have a scaling plan that lets your deck deal 300+ damage over a long fight, spend Act 3 removing your worst cards so a disaster draw is impossible, hold potions (Gambler's Brew, block potions) for the boss, and spend leftover gold on relics that fix your deck's worst draw. If you're dying in Act 3, the cause is almost always too-cautious play in Acts 1–2 — not the boss fight itself.
