18:T11e3,<p>Slay the Spire 2 launches Early Access with <strong>five playable characters</strong>: three returning from the first game (Ironclad, Silent, Defect) and two brand-new ones (Regent, Necrobinder). Each has its own card pool, relics and a mechanical identity that actually changes how you draft. Here&#39;s what every class plays like, with links to the full build guides.</p>
<h2>Ironclad — the self-damage scaling bruiser (returning)</h2>
<p>The Ironclad remains the straightforward starter: trade health for value, stack Strength, and out-scale the fight. In Slay the Spire 2 his identity shifts in two notable ways:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Vulnerability is a resource, not just a debuff.</strong> Cards like <strong>Dominate</strong> gain Strength for every Vulnerability on the enemy, which turns old vuln-appliers (Bash, Tremble, Molten Fist) into enablers for a scaling engine.</li>
<li><strong>Strikes are worth keeping.</strong> With enchantments (his Strikes can be made free) and self-synergies, the Ironclad wants his starting deck more than any other character.</li>
</ul>
<p>Full card-by-card rankings and two complete build paths: <a href="/builds/builds-ironclad">Ironclad Builds</a>.</p>
<h2>Silent — the shiv-and-evasion specialist (returning)</h2>
<p>The Silent still wins by doing everything at once: discard-tank, poison, and a dense shiv package. The current card pool leans hard into <strong>draw-and-discard manipulation</strong> — cards like <strong>Well-Laid Plans</strong>, <strong>Tools of the Trade</strong>, <strong>Calculated Gamble</strong> and <strong>Prepared</strong> sit in S/A tier alongside the usual <strong>Footwork</strong>-powered evasion. Build guide: <a href="/builds/builds-silent">Silent Build</a>.</p>
<h2>Defect — the orb engine (returning)</h2>
<p>The Defect&#39;s orb framework is intact — channel, evoke, and scale with focus — and the S tier of the current card table reads like an orb checklist: <strong>Echo Form</strong>, <strong>Defragment</strong>, <strong>Glacier</strong>, <strong>Loop</strong>, <strong>Capacitor</strong>, <strong>Overclock</strong>, <strong>Ice Lance</strong> and <strong>Chill</strong>. Defragment-style focus stacking plus the new <strong>Machine Learning</strong> and <strong>Genetic Algorithm</strong> powers make the late game extremely snowbally. Build guide: <a href="/builds/builds-defect">Defect Builds</a>.</p>
<h2>Regent — the star-forging sovereign (new)</h2>
<p>The Regent is arguably the strongest of the new characters, and the flashiest: a second resource pool called <strong>Stars</strong> that, unlike Energy, <strong>carries over between turns</strong>. Every turn is a conversion problem — turn Energy into Stars, Stars into damage, block, or draw.</p>
<p>The Regent has two main archetypes:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Star Engine</strong> — generate more stars than you spend (Glow, Big Bang, Royal Gamble, Child of the Stars, Genesis) and spend them on finishers like Comet and Radiate. The consistent, high-Ascension pick.</li>
<li><strong>Sovereign Blade</strong> — Forge a signature sword all run and end bosses with one swing. Huge single-target damage, but less consistent and weaker against AoE fights.</li>
</ul>
<p>Build guide: <a href="/builds/builds-regent">Regent Builds</a>.</p>
<h2>Necrobinder — the lich with a giant hand (new)</h2>
<p>The Necrobinder fights as a <strong>duo-unit</strong>: a fragile spellcaster plus <strong>Osty</strong>, a massive reanimated skeletal hand. Her starting relic, Osty&#39;s Binding, summons it. Two archetypes:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Doom Deck</strong> — mark enemies with Doom stacks; anyone at or below their mark is executed at the end of their turn. Higher card numbers, but you must survive until the clock ticks.</li>
<li><strong>Osty Deck</strong> — grow the hand with Summon effects and let it tank for you; Osty&#39;s health doesn&#39;t wear off like Block.</li>
</ul>
<p>Build guide: <a href="/builds/builds-overview-necrobinder">Necrobinder Builds</a>.</p>
<h2>Which One Should You Start With?</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>First run:</strong> Ironclad or Silent — the most forgiving power curves.</li>
<li><strong>First 20 hours:</strong> Defect, to learn the orb timing rhythm.</li>
<li><strong>After that:</strong> Regent for the highest ceiling, Necrobinder for the most unusual turns.</li>
</ul>
<p>Every character&#39;s full card table (S through D) lives on the <a href="/cards/tier-list">Tier List</a>, and all build pages are linked from the <a href="/builds/builds-overview">Builds overview</a>.</p>
