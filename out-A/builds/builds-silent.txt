19:T3baa,<p>The Silent is back as the agile &quot;Rogue&quot; of the Spire — a master of a thousand cuts and silent toxins who controls fights with superior card draw and debilitating debuffs. Slay the Spire 2 gives her one brand-new keyword, <strong>Sly</strong>, which the community guide calls probably the most powerful mechanic on any character, and this guide covers her three kill routes: the <strong>Sly discard engine</strong> (best by far), <strong>Shiv spam</strong>, and <strong>Poison inevitability</strong> — plus the block-and-velocity core that every Silent deck is built on.</p>
<h2>How the Silent Works</h2>
<p><strong>Sly</strong> reads &quot;if this would be discarded, play it instead.&quot; Functionally, discarding a Sly card is like gaining free Energy and a free card play: if Acrobatics makes you discard a Haze, the Haze simply fires for free on its way to the discard pile. Calculated Gamble with Sly in hand &quot;plays most of the cards in your hand for free and then draws seven.&quot; Any card that says &quot;discard a card&quot; becomes pure upside, and the video guide suspects the mechanic will be nerfed — Acrobatics has already moved to uncommon on the beta branch.</p>
<p><strong>Poison</strong> is damage over time, and it ticks at the start of the enemy&#39;s turn — which means statuses like Beckon or Infection still hurt you on end of turn even if the poison is about to kill the enemy, and since Poison is itself a status, Artifact blocks it. Its math is the reason to care: 7 Poison deals its triangle number, 7+6+5+4+3+2+1 = 28 total damage, and &quot;as far as I&#39;m aware, there&#39;s nothing that does 28 damage unupgraded for two&quot; — the catch is the fight must last seven turns. Poison upgrades are deceptively strong: Snake Bite going from 7 to 10 Poison nearly doubles its total damage (27 in the first three turns versus 18). Accelerant doesn&#39;t raise the total — it cashes the latent damage faster.</p>
<p><strong>Shivs</strong> are 0-cost attacks that deal 4 damage and Exhaust. Two relics are explicitly called run-defining for her: <strong>Shuriken</strong> and <strong>Kunai</strong>, which grant +1 Strength or +1 Dexterity for every 3 attacks played in a turn — on the Silent that turns a Blade Dance into 12 damage plus a Strength, or 10 damage plus a Dexterity.</p>
<p>Know the starter deck: the Silent begins with an <strong>extra Strike and Defend</strong>, plus Ring of the Snake (2 extra cards on turn one), Neutralize and Survivor. That makes her one of the tankiest starters but starved for damage, so early damage cards are a top priority. You can&#39;t realistically remove all the Strikes, so pick one of two plans: grind removals, or lean into her best-in-game card draw and build a bigger 30–40 card deck where Strikes barely register. Mobalytics lists her strengths as a fast multi-card playstyle, easy Weak and block scaling, and Retain/draw consistency; her weaknesses are discard complexity for new players, unreliable energy generation, and low early damage. One structural note that surprises veterans: the Silent effectively has <strong>one archetype with variations</strong> — the best decks mix all of the styles below, and 46% of her card pool is playable in any deck, so the real risk is taking too many good cards.</p>
<h2>Build 1: The Sly Discard Engine (Best Route)</h2>
<p><strong>Play pattern:</strong> stack discard enablers and Sly payoffs until &quot;discard a card&quot; means &quot;play a card for free.&quot; The video guide is blunt: &quot;right now Sly is just so good that there&#39;s almost no reason to do anything else.&quot;</p>

<p><strong>Strengths and weaknesses:</strong> the strongest plan on arguably the current best character; the only real failure mode is lacking discard enablers early — Haze in particular is a bad card in Act 1 without them.</p>

Priority one is defense: Footwork, Abrasive, Leg Sweep, Shadow Meld and Well-Laid Plans come before any Poison card.</p>

<h2>The Universal Core: Block, Dexterity and Card Velocity</h2>
<p>Every Silent deck mixes the routes above onto the same defensive skeleton. These cards go in essentially any build — &quot;you&#39;re really never turning down Adrenaline&quot;:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Footwork</strong> — stack Dexterity until nothing hits you (&quot;you can take a lot of these and just become invincible; once you&#39;re invincible, you can kill with whatever&quot;).</li>
<li><strong>After Image</strong> — Block for every card played; stacks in multiples and turns card spam into full Blocks (one Defend blocking for 24 has happened).</li>
<li><strong>Well-Laid Plans</strong> — retention that deletes draw variance; retain Leg Sweep (11 Block, 2 Weak — &quot;essentially means you take no damage&quot;) for the boss&#39;s attack turn.</li>
<li><strong>Tools of the Trade / Adrenaline / Escape Plan</strong> — velocity and free card plays; &quot;with two Tools of the Trade you could generally win runs without playing any cards.&quot;</li>
<li><strong>Untouchable</strong> — &quot;so much better than it looks&quot;: effectively a zero-Energy Block (9 on main branch per the video, 6 after the beta nerf — either way, Prepared into it is free mitigation).</li>
<li><strong>Piercing Wail</strong> — Weak for multi-hit enemies; take the first two or three, it self-Exhausts.</li>
<li><strong>The Hunt</strong> — more card rewards, and using it on a boss grants double rare rewards; &quot;if you get it at any time in Act 1, just take it.&quot;</li>
<li><strong>Malaise</strong> — buys enormous amounts of time when set up big.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Which Route Should You Pick?</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Sly</strong> whenever the enablers appear — it&#39;s simply the strongest plan and merges with everything.</li>
<li><strong>Shivs</strong> if early rares (Fan of Knives, Knife Trap) show up, ideally alongside Shuriken.</li>
<li><strong>Poison</strong> when your drafts skew defensive — it turns &quot;I can&#39;t die&quot; into a win condition.</li>
<li>In practice, expect every run to blend all three; that&#39;s how the character is designed.</li>
</ul>
